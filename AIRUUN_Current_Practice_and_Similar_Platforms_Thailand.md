# AIRUUN Current Practice & Similar Platforms in Thailand
## Market Benchmark, Comparison and Strategic Implications

**Prepared for:** AIRUUN Pitching Proposal  
**Purpose:** Support the proposal with current practices, comparable platforms in Thailand, market gaps, and implications for AIRUUN’s product and technical direction.  
**Reference date:** July 2026

---

# 1. Executive Summary

Thailand already has several digital platforms addressing parts of the wellbeing ecosystem, including:

- Insurance-linked wellness and rewards
- Insurance customer-service super apps
- Digital healthcare and telemedicine
- Public-health content ecosystems
- Mental-wellness and peer-support platforms
- Active-ageing communities
- Structured digital behaviour-change programmes

However, no visible public benchmark currently combines all of the following into one coherent relationship platform:

- Thai wisdom and cultural grounding
- Multi-dimensional whole-life wellbeing
- Insurer-sponsored but non-sales experience
- Guest, member and policyholder journeys
- Personalized content and guided practices
- Physical programmes and events
- Community participation
- LINE and CRM continuity
- Long-term relationship measurement

This creates a potential white space for AIRUUN.

> **AIRUUN can bring together what currently exists separately in Thailand: trusted wisdom, everyday wellbeing actions, meaningful programmes, physical experiences and a community built around care—not sales.**

AIRUUN should not attempt to copy one existing platform. It should combine selected best practices from multiple models while maintaining its own principles:

- Value before identity
- Relationship before monetization
- No selling inside the core experience
- No dark patterns or engagement farming
- Reading-only users remain valid users
- Digital and physical experiences should feel connected

# 2. Current Practices in Thailand

## 2.1 Insurance-linked Wellness and Rewards

### Benchmark: AIA Vitality / AIA+

AIA Vitality is one of the clearest insurance-linked wellness models in Thailand.

The model combines:

- Health-related activity tracking
- Points and status progression
- Rewards and partner benefits
- Health assessments and challenges
- Connections between healthy behaviour and selected insurance benefits
- Policy servicing and wellness features through AIA+

### Strengths

- Strong member identity
- Measurable behaviour loop
- Clear incentive to return
- Health-data and activity integration
- Large partner ecosystem
- Existing policyholder relationship
- Strong link between online activity and external benefits

### Limitations Relative to AIRUUN

- Wellbeing is heavily centred on physical health and measurable activity
- Motivation relies significantly on points, status and rewards
- Relationship is clearly linked to insurance products
- Limited visible emphasis on Thai wisdom, family relationships, financial balance and contribution to others
- Community is less central than activity tracking and partner benefits

### Lesson for AIRUUN

AIRUUN can learn from:

- Recognized member continuity
- Partner ecosystem design
- Activity and behaviour tracking
- Measurable progression
- Cross-channel member services

AIRUUN should avoid:

- Making rewards the primary reason to participate
- Turning wellbeing into competition
- Overusing streaks, status or loss-aversion mechanics
- Making the platform feel like a route to insurance incentives

## 2.2 Insurance Customer-service Super Apps

### Benchmarks

- AIA+
- MTL Click
- FWD Omne

These platforms generally combine customer services such as:

- Policy information
- Premium payment
- Claims tracking
- Benefits
- Customer support
- Health-related services
- Telemedicine or specialist support
- Campaigns and selected wellbeing services

### Strengths

- Clear reason for customers to log in
- Strong identity and policy-data connection
- Familiar service utility
- Reduced friction across customer-service tasks
- Ability to connect to insurer CRM and operational systems

### Limitations Relative to AIRUUN

- Journeys usually begin with the policy or service need
- Transaction and convenience remain the central value
- Wellbeing content is usually a supporting feature
- Public guest experience is limited
- Community, cultural meaning and participation are not usually core

### Lesson for AIRUUN

AIRUUN should not duplicate policy servicing.

The clearest distinction is:

> **Insurance service applications manage the policy. AIRUUN supports the life around it.**

AIRUUN should connect with insurer systems where useful, while maintaining a different emotional and functional role.

## 2.3 Specialist Digital Healthcare Platform

### Benchmark: Doctor Anywhere Thailand

Doctor Anywhere provides access to healthcare and wellness services through a digital platform.

Visible capabilities include:

- Video consultation
- General healthcare services
- Mental-wellness consultation
- Health screening
- Wellness testing
- Marketplace and health products
- Corporate-health programmes
- Professional booking and payment

### Strengths

- Clear practical utility
- Strong expert and clinical network
- Booking and service-delivery flow
- Professional governance
- Online-to-offline service connection
- Repeat-service relationship

### Limitations Relative to AIRUUN

- Primarily a healthcare-service platform
- Journeys often begin when a user has a medical or clinical need
- Transaction and service delivery are central
- Community and belonging are not primary
- Thai wisdom and whole-life wellbeing are not defining themes

### Lesson for AIRUUN

AIRUUN should include:

- Expert validation
- Clear referral boundaries
- Programme booking where appropriate
- Professional support pathways
- Safety and evidence standards

AIRUUN should not become:

- A telemedicine platform
- A diagnostic tool
- A treatment platform
- A replacement for qualified medical or mental-health services

---

## 2.4 Public-health Ecosystem and Resource Discovery

### Benchmark: ThaiHealth Portal

ThaiHealth Portal acts as an ecosystem entry point for health-related applications, websites and partner resources.

The platform supports:

- Discovery of health resources
- Multiple health topics
- Different audience groups
- Public-benefit content
- Partner-network access
- Connections to external services

### Strengths

- Broad public-health coverage
- Strong institutional credibility
- Large network of partners
- Multiple target groups
- Public access and social value
- Extensive resource ecosystem

### Limitations Relative to AIRUUN

- More similar to a discovery portal than a single member journey
- User experience may be distributed across multiple external platforms
- Identity and progress may not continue across services
- Personalization is not visibly central
- Community identity is not the main organizing principle

### Lesson for AIRUUN

AIRUUN can adopt:

- Partner ecosystem thinking
- Credible content sourcing
- Topic breadth
- Public access
- Links to specialized services

AIRUUN should avoid becoming only a directory.

AIRUUN’s difference should be:

- One member identity
- One shared taxonomy
- One continuous journey
- One experience connecting content, programmes and physical participation

---

## 2.5 Community-first Healthy Ageing

### Benchmark: YoungHappy

YoungHappy is a strong benchmark for active ageing, learning, community and offline participation.

Its visible model includes:

- Online community
- Offline activities
- Courses and learning
- Useful content
- Social connection
- Volunteer and contribution opportunities
- Purpose-driven healthy ageing

### Strengths

- Clear target audience
- Strong community purpose
- Online-to-offline experience
- Members can contribute, not only consume
- Learning and activity are connected
- Belonging and meaning are visible outcomes
- Social contribution is part of the experience

### Limitations Relative to AIRUUN

- Primarily focused on older adults
- Does not visibly support the same breadth of life stages
- No insurer recognition or CRM relationship
- No five-pillar wellbeing structure
- Member journeys may not extend across all wellbeing dimensions

### Lesson for AIRUUN

YoungHappy is one of the closest benchmarks for:

- Community
- Physical activities
- Participation
- Meaning
- Contribution
- Active ageing

AIRUUN should learn that community does not need to begin with an open forum.

It can begin with:

- Events
- Learning
- Small groups
- Volunteer programmes
- Member stories
- Shared purpose

## 2.6 Safe Peer-support Community

### Benchmark: Sati App

Sati App focuses on mental wellbeing, peer support, safe listening and psychological first aid.

Visible characteristics include:

- Safe sharing
- Access to trained listeners
- Peer support
- Mental-wellness education
- Volunteer participation
- Reduced barriers to early support

### Strengths

- Clear emotional need
- Strong human component
- Psychological safety
- Accessible support before clinical treatment
- Community and volunteering dimension
- Purpose is easy to understand

### Limitations Relative to AIRUUN

- Focused mainly on mental wellbeing
- Requires substantial training, safeguarding and operational support
- Does not cover body, family, money and contribution as one whole-life system
- Human-support scaling creates operational cost and risk

### Lesson for AIRUUN

If AIRUUN enables participation in sensitive topics, it will require:

- Trained moderators
- Clear escalation protocols
- Response service levels
- Crisis boundaries
- Sensitive-content consent
- Referral routes
- Community guidelines
- Incident ownership

A safe community cannot be created through technology alone.

## 2.7 Structured Digital Behaviour-change Programme

### Benchmark: WHO Step-by-Step Thailand

Thailand has adapted WHO’s Step-by-Step programme as part of the national digital mental-health platform.

The model includes:

- A structured five-week journey
- Evidence-based exercises
- Interactive content
- Trained e-Helpers
- Ongoing encouragement
- Programme completion
- Support for people experiencing early stress or depression

### Strengths

- Clear beginning and end
- Evidence-based sequence
- Human-supported continuity
- Progress can be measured
- Not dependent on an endless content feed
- Strong programme logic

### Limitations Relative to AIRUUN

- Focused on a specific mental-health use case
- More structured and clinical than general lifestyle content
- Requires trained support and governance
- Not intended as a broad lifestyle and community platform

### Lesson for AIRUUN

AIRUUN should develop guided journeys, not only content cards.

Potential examples:

- Calm in 7 Days
- Better Energy in 7 Days
- Family Conversation Journey
- Monthly Money Reflection
- One Good Act a Week

Each journey should define:

- Entry need
- Sequence
- Duration
- Completion
- Next action
- Optional human or physical support
- Measurement

# 6. What Current Platforms Commonly Do Well

## 6.1 Clear Utility

Strong platforms usually answer immediately:

- What can I do here?
- What will I receive?
- What problem will this help me solve?

Examples include:

- Track health
- Earn rewards
- Consult a doctor
- Talk to a listener
- Join an activity
- Complete a programme
- Learn a skill

### Implication for AIRUUN

AIRUUN should not begin with a long brand story alone.

The first experience should provide a clear and useful action, such as:

- A three-minute practice
- A reflection question
- A family conversation prompt
- A simple money check-in
- A programme recommendation

---

## 6.2 Focused Audience or Need

Strong platforms often have a clear focus:

- Sati: safe listening and mental wellness
- YoungHappy: active ageing
- Doctor Anywhere: healthcare access
- AIA Vitality: healthy behaviour
- Step-by-Step: structured mental-health support

### Implication for AIRUUN

Although AIRUUN covers five pillars, users should enter through one current need.

The user should not be required to understand the complete AIRUUN ecosystem before receiving value.

---

## 6.3 Human or Physical Component

Platforms that build deeper relationships often extend beyond content.

Examples include:

- Professional consultation
- e-Helpers
- Peer listeners
- Community activities
- Courses
- Volunteer programmes
- Partner gyms
- Physical workshops

### Implication for AIRUUN

AIRUUN Physical Space should not be treated as a separate information page.

It should become part of the journey:

1. Discover a need
2. Receive a digital experience
3. Join a physical programme
4. Record attendance
5. Continue digitally
6. Return or contribute

---

## 6.4 Structured Continuity

Strong platforms create a reason to continue through:

- Points and status
- A multi-week programme
- Repeat courses
- Appointments and follow-up
- Saved progress
- Human encouragement

### Implication for AIRUUN

AIRUUN content should not end with only:

> Read another article.

It should offer a useful next step, such as:

- Repeat tomorrow
- Save this practice
- Join an event
- Continue the journey
- Ask for a reminder
- Share a reflection
- Participate in a programme

# 7. Common Gaps in Current Practice

## 7.1 Wellness Is Often Reduced to Physical Health

Many platforms focus on:

- Steps
- Exercise
- Health checks
- Treatment
- Nutrition
- Rewards

This leaves limited room for:

- Family relationships
- Caregiving
- Financial confidence
- Meaning
- Contribution
- Cultural wisdom

### AIRUUN Opportunity

Use the five pillars as a whole-life wellbeing model:

- Jai
- Kai
- Katanyu
- Por-piang
- Nam-jai

---

## 7.2 Content and Services Are Fragmented

Users often move between:

- Websites
- Applications
- LINE
- Event forms
- Partner services
- Physical venues

Identity, preferences and progress may not continue across those touchpoints.

### AIRUUN Opportunity

Create a shared foundation:

- One identity
- One taxonomy
- One member profile
- One journey model
- One analytics framework
- One digital-to-physical relationship

---

## 7.3 Community Is Either Missing or Operationally Heavy

Some platforms have no community.

Others require significant investment in:

- Moderation
- Human support
- Safeguarding
- Programme management
- Incident handling
- Member guidelines

### AIRUUN Opportunity

Begin with curated participation instead of an open forum.

Recommended progression:

1. Read, watch and attend
2. Save and complete a practice
3. React, answer or submit privately
4. Share a curated story
5. Join a small group
6. Volunteer or contribute
7. Lead an activity when governance is ready

---

## 7.4 Insurer Wellness Often Links to Products or Rewards

Insurance-linked wellness commonly uses:

- Points
- Premium benefits
- Status
- Partner rewards
- Policy-service integration

These can create strong motivation, but may also reinforce the idea that wellbeing exists to support insurance or loyalty objectives.

### AIRUUN Opportunity

Separate the core wellbeing experience from product selling.

TGH should be transparent as sponsor, but AIRUUN should lead with member value.

---

## 7.5 Thai Wisdom Is Usually Content, Not Product Logic

Thai wisdom often appears as:

- Articles
- Museum or cultural content
- Campaign storytelling
- One-time events

It is less commonly connected to:

- Daily practices
- Recommendation
- Guided journeys
- Seasonal programming
- Intergenerational participation
- Member identity

### AIRUUN Opportunity

Translate Thai wisdom into product experiences:

- Daily Dawn
- Short practice
- Seasonal rhythm
- Heritage journey
- Physical workshop
- Elder story
- Family activity
- Community contribution

# 9. Strategic Implications for AIRUUN

## 9.1 AIRUUN Should Be a Relationship Platform

It should not be positioned as:

- Another insurer application
- A campaign microsite
- A content portal
- A telemedicine platform
- A reward programme
- An open social network

It should be positioned as:

> **A trusted Wisdom + Wellbeing relationship platform that supports everyday life through useful content, practices, programmes and community participation.**

## 9.2 AIRUUN Should Lead with Immediate Value

The first session should allow a guest to:

- Understand the purpose
- Receive one useful experience
- Select a current need
- Continue without pressure
- Register only when continuity becomes valuable

## 9.3 AIRUUN Should Connect Digital and Physical Experiences

Digital and physical experiences should share:

- Pillar
- Member identity
- Programme information
- Booking or registration
- Attendance
- Follow-up
- Recommendation
- Measurement

## 9.4 AIRUUN Should Start with Guided Participation

Phase 1 should focus on:

- Content
- Practices
- Events
- Save and reminder
- Polls or private reflection
- Curated stories
- Moderated contribution

An open forum should only be considered after governance and operating capacity are proven.

## 9.5 AIRUUN Should Use Rule-based Personalization Before AI

Phase 1 recommendation signals can include:

- Selected wellbeing need
- Primary pillar
- Viewed content
- Saved content
- Event interest
- Preferred format
- Editorial priority

AI should be considered later when AIRUUN has:

- Structured metadata
- Sufficient interaction data
- Clear objectives
- Consent
- Safety rules
- Feedback loops
- Human governance

The project must define:

- Platform positioning

- Policyholder recognition
- Content taxonomy
- Guided journeys

- Physical-programme journey
- Community maturity
- Expert validation
- Identity and consent
- Analytics and learning
- CMS and editorial operation
- System integrations
- Roadmap and optional modules

This is more than a UX/UI or development task.

It requires a coordinated definition across:

- Business Development
- Business Analysis
- System Analysis
- Technology
- UX/UI
- Content operation
- Community operation
- Data and analytics

# 11. Final Strategic Conclusion

Thailand already has strong examples of:

- Wellness rewards
- Policy-service applications
- Digital healthcare
- Public-health ecosystems
- Mental-wellness support
- Active-ageing communities
- Structured behaviour-change programmes

The opportunity for AIRUUN is not to compete directly with one of these platforms.

The opportunity is to combine selected best practices into a new relationship model:

- The identity continuity of an insurer platform
- The credibility of a public-health ecosystem
- The human connection of a safe support community
- The online-to-offline participation of an active-ageing platform
- The structure of a guided behaviour-change programme
- The cultural grounding of Thai wisdom
- The breadth of whole-life wellbeing
- The trust of a non-sales experience

> **AIRUUN should not be the platform that publishes the most content. It should be the platform that helps members take the most meaningful next action.**
