# AIRUUN Strategic Partner Working Brief
## Research, Responsibilities, and Required Definition

**Status:** Working document for strategic-partner alignment  
**Purpose:** Define what the strategic partner must research, decide, and deliver so TWF can translate AIRUUN into a scalable digital product.  
**Source backbone:** `AIRUUN_Internal_Team_Working_Brief.md`

---

# 1. Strategic Partner Mandate

AIRUUN is not being developed as a conventional campaign microsite. It is intended to become a **Wisdom + Wellbeing Platform** that creates long-term relationship value through content, practices, programmes, community participation, and a connected physical experience.

TWF is responsible for turning this direction into a functioning product: information architecture, user journeys, functional requirements, UX/UI, identity, CMS, integrations, analytics, security, development planning, and estimation.

The strategic partner should therefore own the layer that gives the product **meaning, coherence, credibility, and operational life**.

## Strategic Partner Primary Role

The strategic partner should define:

1. What AIRUUN means in the lives of members
2. How the five pillars become an actionable editorial and programme system
3. What members receive daily, weekly, monthly, and seasonally
4. How AIRUUN earns trust without becoming a disguised sales channel
5. How digital content connects with AIRUUN’s physical space
6. How participation grows safely from reading to contribution
7. Who creates, validates, approves, and operates each experience
8. How success is evaluated beyond traffic and lead generation

## Boundary Between Strategic Partner and TWF

| Area | Strategic Partner Leads | TWF Leads |
|---|---|---|
| Core proposition | Meaning, promise, narrative | Translation into product structure |
| Five pillars | Strategic meaning and boundaries | Taxonomy, metadata, navigation, recommendation rules |
| Content | Editorial strategy, formats, cadence, owners | CMS model, templates, publishing workflow |
| Daily Dawn | Editorial concept and programme logic | Product flow, scheduling, archive, tracking |
| Community | Participation model, guidelines, moderation policy | Features, permissions, tools, data model |
| Physical space | Programme concept and operating journey | Listing, booking, reminders, check-in integration |
| Member relationship | Tone, invitation principles, value exchange | Guest/member states, identity, personalization |
| Measurement | Outcomes and learning questions | Event tracking, dashboards, data implementation |
| Governance | Decision rights and approval model | System roles, access control, audit trail |
| Technology | Strategic requirements and constraints | Architecture, implementation, security, integration |

# 2. Non-negotiable Strategic Principles

The strategic partner should use the following principles as design guardrails:

1. **AIRUUN is a gift, not a product.**
2. **Value must be given before identity or participation is requested.**
3. **No selling inside the core wellbeing experience.**
4. **The platform should not use dark patterns, addictive loops, or engagement farming.**
5. **Reading-only users are still successful users.**
6. **AIRUUN leads the experience; TGH is visible and transparent as sponsor.**
7. **The tone should feel warm, plain, unhurried, practical, and respectful.**
8. **Digital and physical AIRUUN should feel like one connected offer.**

These principles should be converted into explicit editorial and programme rules, not left as brand language only.

# 3. Research Foundation

## 3.1 Digital Wellbeing Requires More Than Technology

The World Health Organization’s digital-health strategy states that digital initiatives need coordinated **financial, organisational, human, and technological resources**. This supports the view that AIRUUN cannot succeed through platform development alone; it requires an operating model, credible content owners, governance, and ongoing programme management.

### Implication

The strategic partner must define the human system behind the platform:

- Editorial team
- Expert network
- Programme curators
- Community managers
- Event operators
- Moderators
- Approval owners
- Measurement and learning owners

---

## 3.2 Social Connection Is a Wellbeing Outcome

WHO describes social connection through three dimensions:

- **Structure:** how many relationships and interactions people have
- **Function:** what support is exchanged
- **Quality:** whether relationships are positive and satisfying

WHO also reports that stronger social connection can support mental and physical health and strengthen communities.

### Implication

AIRUUN Community should not be measured only through comments or follower counts. The partner should design participation that improves:

- Quality of interaction
- Mutual support
- Sense of belonging
- Shared activity
- Contribution to others
- Continued connection beyond a single event

---

## 3.3 Financial Wellbeing Is Broader Than Financial Knowledge

OECD defines financial literacy as awareness, knowledge, skills, attitudes, and behaviours that support sound financial decisions and wellbeing. OECD’s more recent financial-wellbeing work also considers people’s ability to manage obligations, absorb shocks, and maintain confidence about their financial future.

### Implication

Por-piang should not become a library of generic money tips or a route into financial-product promotion. It should combine:

- Knowledge
- Reflection
- Small actions
- Behaviour support
- Confidence
- Appropriate referral boundaries

---

## 3.4 Coherent Experiences Need Explicit Classification

WHO’s classification of digital health interventions demonstrates the value of clearly defining what a digital intervention does, for whom, and through which service or workflow.

### Implication

Every AIRUUN content item, practice, programme, and community action should have a defined purpose and metadata. The strategic partner must supply meaning and editorial distinctions; TWF will encode them into CMS and product logic.

# 4. Strategic Workstream A — Platform Proposition and Narrative

## Objective

Turn the broad idea of “Wisdom + Wellbeing” into a proposition that members can understand immediately.

## Required Research

The strategic partner should validate:

- What wellbeing means in the context of Thai life
- What people expect from a wellbeing platform sponsored by an insurer
- What creates suspicion of sales intent
- Which needs feel appropriate for TGH to support
- Which needs feel too sensitive or intrusive
- How AIRUUN differs from media, wellness apps, hospital content, CSR, and loyalty programmes
- What role Thai wisdom and Wat Arun heritage should play in the everyday experience

## Key Questions

1. What useful promise can AIRUUN make in one sentence?
2. What should a member feel after the first 60 seconds?
3. Why should someone return tomorrow?
4. Why is AIRUUN credible in each pillar?
5. What should AIRUUN never become?
6. How should TGH sponsorship be expressed without dominating the experience?

## Required Deliverables

- One-sentence member proposition
- Platform narrative
- Promise and proof framework
- AIRUUN vs TGH brand relationship rule
- “What AIRUUN is / is not” definition
- Member value exchange
- Strategic guardrails
- Tone-of-voice principles
- Language glossary for Thai and English terms
- Narrative hierarchy for homepage, invitation, onboarding, rooms, and physical space

## Acceptance Criteria

The proposition should:

- Be understandable without explaining insurance strategy
- Lead with member value
- Work for policyholders and non-policyholders
- Avoid abstract wellness language
- Remain valid across content, events, and community
- Clearly separate education and wellbeing from selling

# 5. Strategic Workstream B — Five-Pillar Content System

## Objective

Translate Jai, Kai, Katanyu, Por-piang, and Nam-jai from strategic themes into a usable content and programme system.

## Required Definition for Every Pillar

For each pillar, define:

1. Human need addressed
2. Scope and boundaries
3. Typical member situations
4. Intended emotional outcome
5. Appropriate content formats
6. Appropriate practices and programmes
7. Relevant experts and sources
8. Safety or compliance boundaries
9. Physical-space opportunities
10. Community participation opportunities
11. Example journeys
12. Measurement signals

## Pillar Definition Template

### Jai — The Heart

Define boundaries among:

- Everyday stress
- Emotional reflection
- Rest and resilience
- Mental-health education
- Clinical mental-health support

Clarify when content can remain general and when professional referral is needed.

### Kai — The Body

Define boundaries among:

- Movement
- Sleep
- Energy
- Nutrition
- Healthy ageing
- Preventive health
- Medical advice

Clarify evidence standards and safety disclaimers for practices.

### Katanyu — Loved Ones

Define:

- Family connection
- Intergenerational relationships
- Caregiving
- Ageing parents
- Communication
- Grief, conflict, and sensitive family issues

Clarify privacy and safeguarding rules for member stories.

### Por-piang — Enough

Define:

- Financial confidence
- Sufficient living
- Planning
- Habits and reflection
- General financial education
- Referral boundaries
- Explicit separation from product sales or personalised financial advice

### Nam-jai — Warmth to Others

Define:

- Kindness
- Generosity
- Volunteering
- Community contribution
- Mutual aid
- Member-led activity
- Recognition without turning generosity into competition

## Required Deliverables

- Pillar strategy cards
- Need-state map
- Topic boundaries
- Expert and source criteria
- Content-format matrix
- Practice and programme matrix
- Safety and escalation rules
- Example monthly content plan
- Example cross-pillar journeys
- Recommended launch content inventory
- Content quality checklist

# 6. Strategic Workstream C — Daily Dawn Editorial Product

## Objective

Define Daily Dawn as a complete, repeatable editorial experience rather than a daily post.

## Strategic Role

Daily Dawn should:

- Give immediate value even without a click
- Create a gentle return rhythm
- Represent all five pillars over time
- Connect daily wisdom to deeper rooms, practices, and programmes
- Feel editorially intentional rather than automated
- Avoid pressure, streak anxiety, and engagement manipulation

## Research Questions

- What time and context are appropriate for delivery?
- Should the tone differ on weekdays, weekends, holidays, or seasons?
- How often should each pillar appear?
- Which formats work inside LINE and which require the platform?
- What makes a Dawn “complete”?
- When should a Dawn include a next action?
- How should personalisation affect selection without creating a filter bubble?
- How long should previous Dawns remain available?

## Proposed Dawn Content Types

- One useful insight
- One reflection question
- One short audio
- One movement practice
- One financial pause
- One family prompt
- One act of kindness
- One piece of Thai wisdom
- One seasonal or cultural observation
- One invitation into a physical programme

## Required Deliverables

- Daily Dawn editorial charter
- Content anatomy
- Format specifications
- Cadence and pillar rotation
- Seasonal calendar
- Editorial selection logic
- Personalisation boundaries
- LINE copy principles
- Archive and reuse policy
- 30-day sample Dawn calendar
- Approval workflow
- Measurement framework

## Recommended Measures

- Dawn delivered
- Dawn viewed
- Completion or play
- Save/share
- Optional next action
- Return within seven days
- Self-reported usefulness
- Distribution balance across pillars

Do not use daily open rate alone as the definition of success.

# 7. Strategic Workstream D — Rooms and Experience Curation

## Objective

Define what users can do inside each room and why each room deserves to exist.

## Initial Rooms

- Practice
- Heritage
- Health Room
- Financial Room
- The Commons
- Live Sessions

## Required Definition per Room

- Purpose
- Member need served
- Core content and activity types
- Entry paths
- Recommended first experience
- Browsing and curation logic
- Relationship to pillars
- Relationship to Daily Dawn
- Relationship to physical space
- Access level: public, registered, recognised member
- Content owner
- Update cadence

## Strategic Guidance

Rooms should be finite, curated destinations—not endless feeds. Each should help a member answer:

1. Why am I here?
2. What can I do now?
3. What might help me next?
4. Can I participate without exposing personal information?
5. How do I continue offline or later?

## Required Deliverables

- Curation rules
- Entry and exit paths
- Cross-room recommendation principles
- Launch readiness checklist
- Room owner and operating cadence

# 8. Strategic Workstream E — Community and Participation Model

## Objective

Create a safe progression from receiving value to contributing value.

## Recommended Maturity Model

### Level 0 — Receive

- Read
- Watch
- Listen
- Attend

### Level 1 — Personal Action

- Save
- Reflect
- Complete a practice
- Set a gentle reminder

### Level 2 — Lightweight Participation

- React
- Answer a poll
- Send thanks
- Submit a private reflection
- Join an activity

### Level 3 — Curated Contribution

- Submit a story
- Ask an expert
- Share a practice
- Volunteer
- Join a small group

### Level 4 — Member-led Participation

- Propose an activity
- Host a session
- Mentor or support others
- Lead a local initiative

AIRUUN should not begin with an open forum. It should begin with controlled, purposeful participation and grow only when governance and moderation capacity are proven.

## Required Research

- What members are willing to share publicly, privately, or anonymously
- Which topics require enhanced moderation
- What motivates contribution without rewards
- How to recognise members without gamifying vulnerability or generosity
- How online participation connects with physical events
- What response time members expect
- What types of misconduct or harmful advice are foreseeable

## Required Deliverables

- Community purpose
- Participation ladder
- Community guidelines
- Moderation policy
- Safeguarding and escalation matrix
- Submission and consent rules
- Member recognition principles
- Moderator roles and service levels
- Pilot community formats
- Member-led activity policy
- Crisis and harmful-content protocol

## Community Measures

- Meaningful participation rate
- Repeat participation
- Quality and safety of interactions
- Member-to-member support
- Contribution diversity
- Response time
- Moderation load
- Event-to-community continuation
- Self-reported belonging

# 10. Strategic Workstream G — Audience, Invitation, and Relationship Design

## Objective

Define how AIRUUN creates value for four user states:

1. Anonymous guest
2. Registered non-policyholder
3. Recognised TGH policyholder
4. Admin, content, and event staff

## Required Research

The partner should test:

- What policyholders assume when TGH invites them
- Whether users expect a sales follow-up
- Which wording reduces sales suspicion
- What value is strong enough to justify registration
- Which experiences should remain public
- Whether policyholder privileges are meaningful or divisive
- Preferred channels and frequencies
- Which needs vary by life stage
- Which content users are comfortable receiving through LINE

## Strategic Principle

Policyholders and non-policyholders should share the same core wellbeing experience. Differences should initially be limited to:

- Recognition
- Continuity
- Communication
- Saved history
- Possible event access

Do not create a lower-quality public experience solely to force registration.

## Required Deliverables

- Audience hypotheses
- Life-stage and need-state framework
- Invitation message system
- Guest value policy
- Registration value exchange
- Policyholder recognition principles
- Channel and frequency principles
- Re-engagement rules
- Consent-language guidance
- Prototype research plan

- Human needs
- Five pillars
- Editorial content
- Daily rituals
- Physical programmes
- Safe participation
- Trust and ethics
- Operating roles

TWF can then translate that system into:

- Information architecture
- Product journeys
- CMS and content model

- Personalisation rules
- Event and community modules
- Analytics

- Development scope and roadmap

> **The strategic partner defines what AIRUUN should mean and how it should live.
